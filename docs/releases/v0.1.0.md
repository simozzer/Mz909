**The first release of Mz909**, an emulation of the Roland TR-909 Rhythm Composer as a VST3 instrument and a standalone app.

![The Mz909 panel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/simozzer/Mz909/v0.1.0/docs/screenshot.png)

### Download

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `Mz909-0.1.0-win64-Setup.exe` | **The installer.** Installs the VST3, the standalone app or both, for everyone or just you, with an uninstaller. Run it, then rescan plugins in your DAW. |
| `Mz909-0.1.0-win64-VST3.zip` | The plugin, to install by hand. Copy `Mz909.vst3` to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Common\VST3` (or `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3`) and rescan in your DAW. |
| `Mz909-0.1.0-win64-Standalone.zip` | `Mz909.exe`, which plays without a DAW. |

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. The installer and binaries aren't code-signed, so Windows may warn about an unknown publisher. Choose *More info → Run anyway*, or build from source.

### What's in it
Half analog, half digital, like the 909 itself:
- **The drums are circuits:** the bass drum, snare, toms, rim shot and clap are modelled as the hardware makes them, not sampled.
- **The kick:** a VCO sweeping down to 50 Hz. TUNE sets how far and how long it falls, and ATTACK sets the beater's click.
- **The metal is 6-bit samples:** hats, crash and ride play from 6-bit ROMs into a 6-bit DAC, as on the hardware, grit included. TUNE plays a cymbal faster and shorter, as the 909's clock does.
- **Accent as the 909 does it:** a harder strike, not a volume boost. An accented kick sweeps from higher.
- **The hats share one DAC and one VCA,** so a closed hat chokes an open one. The VCA has a threshold, so a hat holds, then stops dead.

All eleven sounds, with every knob from the original panel:
- bass drum, snare
- low, mid and hi toms
- rim shot, hand clap
- closed and open hihat, crash, ride

**Playing it:**
- **MIDI:** notes follow the General MIDI drum map, and velocity above 100 is an accent.
- **Pads:** eleven pads on the panel play each sound and light up whenever it is struck.
- **MIDI control:** every knob has a MIDI CC, and you can MIDI-learn your own.
- **Nine factory kits,** and hover hints on every control.

### Known limitations
- **Not the original samples.** Roland's hat and cymbal recordings are Roland's, so Mz909's ROMs hold stand-ins, generated from noise and sine waves and voiced to measurements of a Behringer RD-9. They play exactly as the 909 plays its own, but they are not the 909's metal.
- **No sequencer yet.** Program patterns in your DAW, or play the sounds from a pad controller.
- **One output.** The hardware has an output per instrument; Mz909 mixes them to one for now.
- **Windows only** for now.
- **The drums are not calibrated against a real TR-909.** The structure of each voice is the 909's. Their pitches, times, filters and levels are worked out from the circuits and the sound, not measured from a unit.
